🧭 Buying Guide: How to Choose Between Ninja and Tower

1. Dual Zone vs Single Basket: The Deciding Factor

This is the biggest question. Dual zone cooking (Ninja AF400UK) lets you cook two foods at two different temperatures. If dinner in your house looks like "chicken + vegetables" or "salmon + chips" or "sausages + roast potatoes", you'll use the dual zone every single day. It eliminates the "what's ready first?" juggling act.

Single basket/oven style (Tower T17123, Ninja AF100UK) means everything cooks at one temperature. For most simple meals — chips, nuggets, fish fingers, reheating — that's perfectly fine. But if you want roast chicken (200°C) and roast potatoes (180°C) together, you'll need to cook them sequentially, which takes twice as long.

2. Capacity: How Much Space Do You Really Need?

  • 1–2 people: 3.8L (Ninja AF100UK) is sufficient for most meals
  • 3–4 people: 9.5L dual zone (Ninja AF400UK) or 11L single (Tower T17123) — you need the space for family portions
  • 5+ people / batch cooking: 11L Tower T17123 wins — nothing else fits a whole chicken AND a tray of vegetables

3. Budget Reality Check

At £69, the Tower T17123 is less than half the price of the Ninja AF400UK (£179). If you're unsure whether you'll use an air fryer regularly, the Tower is a low-risk entry point. But if you already know you'll use it daily (and you cook varied meals), the Ninja's dual zone capability is worth every penny of the extra £110.

4. Worktop Space

Measure your available space before buying. The Ninja AF400UK needs 39 × 37 cm of worktop (and ventilation space above). The Tower T17123 is narrower (35 × 32 cm) but taller (38 cm) — it may not fit under low-hanging cabinets. The Ninja AF100UK is the most accommodating at just 24 × 33 cm.
