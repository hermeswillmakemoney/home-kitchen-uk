How to Choose an Air Fryer

Capacity: How Many People Are You Cooking For?

1–2 people: A 4–5L basket is plenty. 3–4 people: Aim for 5–6L — fits a whole chicken. 5+ people: Either a dual-zone model like the Ninja AF400UK or cooking in batches.

Power: Faster Cooking = Higher Wattage

Most air fryers range from 1400W to 2500W. Higher wattage means faster preheating and crispier results. At the current UK energy price cap, running a 1750W air fryer for 30 minutes costs about 21p — versus roughly 50p for a full oven.

Features That Actually Matter

  • Max temperature: 240°C models produce noticeably crispier results than 200°C models.
  • Dishwasher-safe parts: You'll clean it daily — this matters more than Wi-Fi.
  • Preset programs: Nice but not essential. Most people settle on 2–3 temperatures they use regularly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are air fryers cheaper to run than ovens?

Yes — significantly. An air fryer typically uses 1,500–1,800W and cooks food in roughly half the time of a conventional oven. A typical air fryer meal costs 15–25p in electricity versus 40–60p in an oven.

Can you cook raw meat in an air fryer?

Absolutely. Chicken breasts, thighs, sausages, bacon, and even whole roast chickens cook beautifully. Use a meat thermometer to ensure safe internal temperatures.

Do you need to preheat an air fryer?

Most models recommend 2–3 minutes of preheating. We found preheating makes a noticeable difference for frozen foods and anything you want properly crispy. For reheating leftovers, it's not necessary.
