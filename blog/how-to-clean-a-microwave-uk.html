How to Clean a Microwave: The No-Scrub UK Guide

Four methods tested — lemon steam, white vinegar, bicarbonate of soda, and shop-bought cleaners. Plus: how often you should actually clean it, the one part most people forget, and how to get rid of burnt-food smells for good.

📅 14 May 2026 ⏱ 6 min read ✍️ Home & Kitchen UK

🍯 The Short Answer

The easiest and cheapest way to clean a microwave is the lemon steam method: microwave a bowl of water with half a lemon for 3–5 minutes, leave the door shut for another 5, then wipe everything down with a cloth. The steam loosens every bit of dried-on food, and the lemon neutralises smells. For burnt-on smells that won't shift, white vinegar is more effective. For baked-on splatters, bicarbonate of soda paste gives you extra scrubbing power without scratching.

Why a Dirty Microwave Actually Matters

A microwave with last week's curry on the ceiling isn't just unpleasant. It causes real problems:

  • Smells transfer between foods. Heat porridge after someone reheated fish without wiping down first, and your breakfast will taste of it. Grease layers absorb odours, and reheating releases them back into whatever you cook next.
  • Old food grows bacteria. A microwave doesn't sterilise itself. Splatters sitting for weeks in a warm, humid box create exactly the environment bacteria like. Salmonella and E. coli can survive on dried food residue.
  • Your microwave works less efficiently. Grime on the waveguide cover (that small square panel on the interior wall) absorbs microwaves instead of letting them pass into your food, meaning uneven heating and longer cooking times.

How Often Should You Clean a Microwave?

Most UK households clean their microwave far less often than they should. Here's a practical routine that takes almost no time:

Task Frequency Time Required
Wipe down after each use Daily (when splatters occur) 30 seconds
Quick steam clean (lemon/water) Weekly 10 minutes
Deep clean (turntable, door seal, vents) Monthly 20 minutes
Odour removal (vinegar or bicarb) As needed 15 minutes

If you microwave fish, curry, or anything with a strong aroma, clean it immediately afterwards. Those smells set fast.

Method 1: Lemon Steam — The Best All-Rounder

⭐ Best Overall — ~10p per use

🍋 Lemon Steam Method

You need: Half a fresh lemon (or a tablespoon of bottled lemon juice), a microwave-safe bowl, and tap water. Half a lemon is roughly 10–15p; bottled lemon juice (Jif or own-brand) is ~£1 for a 250ml bottle.

  1. Fill a microwave-safe bowl halfway with water. Squeeze half a lemon into it, then drop the lemon half in.
  2. Microwave on high for 3–5 minutes until the water boils and the window steams up. For a grimy microwave, go for 5 minutes.
  3. Leave the door closed for another 5 minutes. The steam continues loosening dried-on food. Do not open early — this is the step that does most of the work.
  4. Remove the bowl carefully (it'll be hot). Take out the turntable plate and roller ring.
  5. Wipe every surface with a damp microfibre cloth. Start with the ceiling, then the walls, then the floor. The steam loosens everything — no scrubbing needed.
  6. Dry with a clean tea towel and replace the turntable.

Cost: ~10p per clean. Best for: Routine weekly cleaning. Light to moderate splatters. Neutralises smells naturally.

💡 Why Lemon Works

The steam is doing the heavy lifting — it condenses on the cooler microwave walls and rehydrates dried-on food, turning it soft enough to wipe away. The lemon juice adds mild citric acid, which cuts through grease and neutralises odour-causing compounds. It's the same chemistry behind citric acid descaling, just gentler.

Method 2: White Vinegar — Best for Burnt-On Smells

💰 Cheapest — ~5p per use

🧾 White Vinegar Steam Method

You need: White distilled vinegar (Sarson's or supermarket own-brand, ~£1.50 for 568ml), a microwave-safe bowl, water. Do not use malt vinegar — it leaves a brown residue and a smell that's harder to shift than whatever you're trying to remove.

  1. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a microwave-safe bowl — roughly 100ml of each.
  2. Microwave on high for 5 minutes, or until the mixture boils and the window is fully steamed.
  3. Leave the door shut for 10 minutes. Vinegar needs longer than lemon to break down heavy grease. The smell will be strong — open a kitchen window.
  4. Wipe down all interior surfaces. Any remaining vinegar smell dissipates within 10–15 minutes of the door being left open.

Cost: ~5p per clean. Best for: Burnt-on smells (curry, fish, popcorn). Heavy grease buildup. Microwaves that haven't been cleaned in months.

Method 3: Bicarbonate of Soda — For Stubborn, Baked-On Splatters

⚡ Best for Tough Stains — ~2p per use

🧪 Bicarb Paste Method

You need: Bicarbonate of soda (any supermarket — ~80p for a 200g tub), water, a sponge or cloth. Bicarb is a mild abrasive that scrubs without scratching the microwave's interior coating.

  1. Make a paste: Mix 2 tablespoons of bicarbonate of soda with just enough water to form a thick, spreadable paste — roughly the consistency of toothpaste.
  2. Apply directly to baked-on splatters with a damp sponge or your finger. Leave it to sit for 5 minutes.
  3. Scrub gently with the rough side of a non-scratch sponge. The bicarb paste will break down the food residue without damaging the surface.
  4. Wipe clean with a damp cloth and dry. Follow up with the lemon steam method to deodorise if needed.

Cost: ~2p per clean. Best for: Specific stubborn spots — tomato sauce splatters, melted cheese, dried-on porridge. Use alongside the steam method for a full clean.

Method 4: Shop-Bought Microwave Cleaners

⏱ Quickest — £1.50–£4 per use

🛒 Commercial Microwave Cleaners

You need: A dedicated microwave cleaner. Options available at UK retailers include: HG Microwave Cleaner (~£4, Tesco/Asda), Dettol Kitchen Spray (~£2.50, most supermarkets), and Method Heavy-Duty Degreaser (~£3.50, Sainsbury's/Waitrose). There are also single-use microwave cleaning sachets (like the Angry Mama steam cleaner, ~£8 from Amazon or Lakeland) — a plastic figurine you fill with vinegar and water, which vents steam through its head.

  1. Spray the interior with the cleaning product, avoiding the waveguide cover and any vent holes.
  2. Leave for the time specified on the bottle — usually 2–5 minutes.
  3. Wipe away with a damp cloth. Some products require a second wipe with clean water.
  4. Run the microwave empty for 30 seconds afterwards if there's any lingering chemical smell.

Cost: £1.50–£4 per use. Best for: When you want it done in under 5 minutes. Products with antibacterial claims if you're concerned about raw meat spills.

⚠️ What NOT to Use in a Microwave

Never use bleach, oven cleaner, wire wool, or metal scourers. Bleach can corrode the interior coating and its fumes are dangerous in a confined space. Oven cleaner is far too caustic and can damage the door seal. Wire wool scratches the interior surface, creating crevices where food and bacteria collect, and metal fragments can cause sparking. Also avoid abrasive cream cleaners like Cif on microwaves with a painted interior — they'll strip the coating over time.

The Parts Most People Forget

It's easy to wipe the interior and call it done, but these areas need attention too:

  • The turntable plate and roller ring. Both go in the dishwasher. Food collects in the grooves of the roller ring and can stop it turning smoothly.
  • The door seal and frame. Wipe the rubber seal and the frame with a damp cloth. A dirty seal can prevent the door from closing properly, which stops the microwave from operating entirely.
  • The waveguide cover. That small square panel on the interior wall (usually on the right). Keep it clean but do not scrub it hard or use anything sharp near it.
  • The vent grilles. Usually on the back or side. Vacuum them with a brush attachment every few months — clogged vents reduce airflow and make the microwave run hotter.

How to Deodorise a Microwave That Still Smells

If you've cleaned the microwave and it still smells of last night's curry, the odour has soaked into the interior surface. Here's how to fix it:

  1. White vinegar soak: Leave a bowl of undiluted white vinegar inside the microwave overnight with the door shut. In the morning, discard the vinegar and leave the door open for an hour to air.
  2. Bicarbonate of soda: Sprinkle a thin layer of dry bicarb across the floor of the microwave and leave for 8–12 hours. Sweep or vacuum it out, then wipe with a damp cloth.
  3. Activated charcoal: Place a small bowl of activated charcoal (available from pet shops or Amazon, ~£5) in the microwave for 24 hours. Charcoal can be reused — place it in sunlight to "recharge" it.

Prevention: Stop Your Microwave Getting Filthy in the First Place

The single best thing you can do is cover your food. A microwave splatter guard costs £3–£8 and stops 90% of splatters reaching the walls. They're available at Wilko, Lakeland, IKEA, and Amazon. If you don't have a cover, a sheet of kitchen roll laid loosely over the bowl works for most things.

Other tips:

  • Wipe immediately, not later. A splash of soup takes 5 seconds while fresh. Leave it until tomorrow and it'll take 5 minutes of scrubbing.
  • Don't overfill bowls. Food that bubbles over the rim causes most microwave mess. Leave at least 2 cm of headroom.
  • Stir liquids halfway through. A stirred soup is far less likely to erupt than one blasted on full power without interruption.

When a Clean Microwave Isn't Enough

If your microwave is clean but still heating unevenly, making unusual noises, or taking longer than it used to, the problem may not be dirt. The magnetron degrades over time — after 7–10 years of daily use, most microwaves start to lose power. A clean microwave won't fix a broken turntable motor, faulty door switch, or blown fuse.
