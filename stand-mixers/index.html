How to Choose the Right Stand Mixer

A stand mixer is a significant investment, and the right choice depends on what you bake, how often, and how much space you have. Here's everything you need to know before buying.

Tilt-Head vs Bowl-Lift: Which Design Is Right for You?

Stand mixers come in two main designs, and each has distinct advantages:

  • Tilt-head mixers (KitchenAid Artisan, Kenwood kMix, Smeg SMF03, VonShef): The head hinges back to give you access to the bowl. These are easier to use, take up less vertical space (better for under-cabinet storage), and are the most common design for home use. All five models in our roundup are tilt-head.
  • Bowl-lift mixers (KitchenAid Heavy Duty, Kenwood Chef XL): The bowl sits on two arms and a lever raises it up to the mixing attachment. These are more stable for very heavy doughs, usually have larger bowls (5.7-6.9L), and suit serious bread makers. However, they require more vertical clearance — you need enough space above the mixer to lift the bowl out.

For most home bakers, a tilt-head model is the better choice. Only consider a bowl-lift if you regularly make large batches of bread or double recipes.

Bowl Capacity: What Can You Actually Make?

Bowl capacity determines how much you can mix in one go. Here's a practical guide to what each size handles:

  • 4.3-4.8 litres: Makes up to 1 standard loaf of bread (1kg dough), a 2-layer cake, or 4 dozen cookies. The KitchenAid Artisan and Smeg SMF03 fall in this range. Good for baking for 2-4 people.
  • 5 litres: Makes up to 1.5 loaves, a 3-layer cake, or 6 dozen cookies. The Kenwood kMix and Sage Bakery Boss are here. Ideal for family baking.
  • 5.5+ litres: Handles double batches, large bread loaves, and big celebration cakes. The VonShef at 5.5L is the largest in our roundup.

A larger bowl is always useful, but bigger mixers take up more worktop space. For most households, 4.8-5L is the sweet spot.

Wattage: More Isn't Always Better

Stand mixer wattage can be misleading. The KitchenAid Artisan has only a 300W motor yet outperforms much higher-wattage mixers on tough doughs. Why? Because gear quality and motor type matter more than raw wattage:

  • DC motors (KitchenAid): Lower wattage, higher torque, quieter, more efficient. Better for sustained heavy mixing.
  • AC motors (most others): Higher wattage needed for the same torque, typically louder, but cheaper to manufacture.

As a rough guide: 300W DC (KitchenAid) performs similarly to 800-1000W AC (Kenwood) on bread dough. The Sage Bakery Boss at 1500W AC is genuinely the most powerful here, but don't dismiss the KitchenAid based on its modest wattage alone.

The Attachment Ecosystem: KitchenAid's Killer Feature

If you think you'll want to use your stand mixer for more than just mixing, KitchenAid has an unassailable advantage. The front hub connection on all KitchenAid mixers accepts a huge range of attachments that turn the mixer into:

  • Pasta maker: 3-piece roller and cutter set (~£120) — makes fresh lasagne sheets, tagliatelle, spaghetti
  • Food processor: Slices, grates, and chops (~£100)
  • Ice cream maker: Freeze bowl attachment (~£85) — makes 1.9L of ice cream
  • Spiralizer: Courgette noodles, apple spirals (~£75)
  • Meat grinder: Fresh mince and sausages (~£90)
  • Grain mill: Fresh flour from whole grains (~£130)
  • Citrus juicer: (~£45)

Kenwood and Sage offer some attachments, but the range is much smaller. Smeg and VonShef have essentially none. If the attachment system matters to you, KitchenAid is the only serious choice.

UK Kitchen Size: These Are Heavy and Permanent

Before buying, be realistic about your kitchen space. Stand mixers weigh between 6.5kg and 10.5kg — the KitchenAid Artisan at 10.5kg is not something you'll want to lug in and out of a cupboard daily. Most people who buy one keep it permanently on the worktop.

Measure your available space carefully, including the height if you plan to store it under wall cabinets. The KitchenAid Artisan is 35cm tall — fine under most cabinets. Tilt-head models need additional clearance above to tilt the head back (about 45cm total).

If you have limited worktop space, the VonShef at 6.5kg is the most practical to move in and out of storage. For permanent worktop placement, weight is actually an advantage — heavier mixers are more stable during kneading.
