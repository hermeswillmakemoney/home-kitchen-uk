Best Kitchen Appliance Deals UK: When to Buy (2026)

Timing is everything. The complete UK sales calendar for kitchen appliances — plus the 'new model' trick, price tracking tools, and where to find genuine bargains on refurbished stock.

📅 13 May 2026 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ Home & Kitchen UK

🏷️ The Short Answer

The best time to buy kitchen appliances in the UK is January (30–50% off across all retailers) followed by Amazon Prime Day in July. Black Friday in November offers genuine savings on previous-generation models — but watch out for 'deals' on current models that were actually cheaper in August. For Ninja, Tower, and Tefal products specifically, the single best strategy is waiting for the new model to launch and buying the superseded version at 20–30% off.

The UK Sales Calendar: Every Key Date for 2026

26 December 2025 – 31 January 2026

🎄 Boxing Day & January Sales

30–50% off

The biggest and most reliable sale of the year. Every major UK retailer — Currys, Argos, John Lewis, AO.com, Amazon UK — clears Christmas stock and last year's models. This is when you'll find the deepest discounts on air fryers, coffee machines, and kettles. John Lewis typically starts its clearance on Christmas Eve online; Currys goes live on Boxing Day at 6am. The best stock goes in the first 48 hours.

Best for: Everything. Premium brands like Smeg, KitchenAid, and Dualit that rarely discount at other times often appear with 20–30% off.

Late March – Early April 2026

📊 End of Financial Year Clearance

15–30% off

Retailers with a March year-end (including many independent appliance shops and some larger chains) clear stock to improve their balance sheets. Less publicised than January, which means less competition for stock. Look for deals on AO.com and Appliances Direct in particular — both run "End of Tax Year" sales that are genuinely worth checking.

May Bank Holidays (5 May & 26 May 2026)

🏦 May Bank Holiday Weekend Sales

10–25% off

Not as deep as January, but worth checking for mid-range small appliances. Currys, Argos, and John Lewis all run Bank Holiday promotions. The early May bank holiday typically has better kitchen appliance deals than the late May one, which tends to focus on garden and DIY.

Mid-July 2026 (exact date TBC — usually 14–15 July)

📦 Amazon Prime Day

20–40% off

Amazon's own devices (Echo, Fire, Ring) get the biggest discounts, but kitchen appliances see genuine savings too. The key dynamic: other retailers price-match Amazon — Argos, Currys, and John Lewis all run counter-Prime Day sales. This means you can often get the same deal from a retailer with better customer service and warranty support. In 2025, the Ninja Foodi MAX dropped to £139 during Prime Day (from £179.99) — a genuine 23% saving.

August Bank Holiday (31 August 2026)

☀️ August Bank Holiday

10–20% off

Generally the weakest of the bank holiday sales for kitchen appliances, but it's worth a skim. Retailers are clearing summer stock (BBQs, garden furniture) and kitchen deals are thinner on the ground. That said, August is often when prices on current models are at their lowest before the pre-Black Friday price hikes begin.

Late November 2026 (28 November = Black Friday)

🛍️ Black Friday & Cyber Monday

15–40% off — but caveats apply

Black Friday in the UK is no longer a single day — it's a two-week event spanning mid-November to Cyber Monday. The genuine bargains are on previous-generation models being cleared to make way for newer versions. Current-year models are often more expensive on Black Friday than they were in August — retailers quietly raise prices in October to make November's 'discounts' look bigger.

How to win at Black Friday: Use CamelCamelCamel or PriceSpy to check the price history of anything you're considering. If the price in August was £129 and the Black Friday 'deal' is £119 (down from an inflated £169), you're saving £10 — not £50. Wait for genuine clearance of superseded models.

The 'New Model' Trick: Save 20–30% on Almost-New Appliances

This is the single most reliable strategy for saving money on kitchen appliances, and almost nobody talks about it. Here's how it works:

When a manufacturer releases a new version of a popular appliance — say, the Ninja Foodi MAX v2 replaces the v1 — retailers need to clear the old stock fast. The v1 is typically 95% as good as the v2 (most upgrades are incremental: a slightly bigger screen, one extra cooking function, a marginally quieter fan). But the price drops by 20–30% almost overnight.

🔍 Recent Real Examples

Ninja Foodi MAX AF400UK → AF451UK: When the AF451 launched at £219.99, the superseded AF400 dropped from £199.99 to £149.99 at Currys and Argos — a 25% saving for a model with near-identical performance.

Instant Pot Duo v5 → Duo v6: The v5 dropped from £79.99 to £54.99 on Amazon when the v6 launched. The difference? A slightly redesigned steam release valve and a new colour option.

How to spot imminent new models: Follow the manufacturer's social media accounts, sign up for their email newsletters, and check tech news sites like TechRadar and Trusted Reviews. When a new model is announced, set a price alert for the old model immediately — it'll drop within 2–4 weeks.

Refurbished and Graded Stock: The Hidden Goldmine

Refurbished appliances have an undeserved reputation for being unreliable. In reality, most are customer returns that were opened, used once or twice, and sent back within the 14-day cooling-off period. Legally, these can't be sold as new — but they're functionally identical and come with warranties.

⚠️ Refurbished Buying Rules

Always check the warranty. Legitimate refurbished goods from AO, eBay Refurbished, and manufacturer outlets always include a minimum 12-month warranty. If a seller offers less, walk away.

Check the return policy. You have the same 14-day right to return under distance selling regulations as you would with a new product. If the product is faulty, the retailer must cover return postage.

Avoid 'seller refurbished' on eBay unless they're a major retailer. Stick to 'certified refurbished' (blue badge) or manufacturer refurbished — these are held to specific standards.

Independent Testing: Trust These Sources

Before buying any kitchen appliance at any price, check independent reviews. These organisations don't accept advertising or free products and test appliances rigorously:

  • Which?: The gold standard for UK product testing. Requires a subscription (£10.99/month) but worth it for a major purchase. Their lab tests for air fryers, kettles, and coffee machines are the most thorough available.
  • Trusted Reviews: Free, detailed, UK-focused. Good for tech-heavy appliances like robot vacuums and smart kettles.
  • Good Housekeeping Institute (GHI): Rigorous testing with a domestic focus. Their kitchen appliance reviews are particularly strong.
  • Home Kitchen UK: We aggregate thousands of verified customer reviews to rank products by real-world performance — not lab conditions. Our rankings are updated monthly.

The One-Minute Deal-Check Checklist

Before clicking 'Buy' on any kitchen appliance deal, run through these five checks:

  1. Check the price history. Paste the URL into CamelCamelCamel or PriceSpy. Was this product actually cheaper 3 months ago? If yes, this isn't a deal — it's marketing.
  2. Check if a new model is imminent. Google "[product name] new model 2026" or "[brand] 2026 lineup." If a replacement is coming, wait 2–4 weeks for the old model to drop.
  3. Check refurbished options. Search the same product on AO Outlet, eBay Refurbished, and Amazon Warehouse. You might find it for 30% less with the same warranty.
  4. Check John Lewis. Even if they're not the cheapest, John Lewis includes a free 2-year guarantee on most electricals — and they'll price-match any UK competitor. You get the lower price and the better warranty.
  5. Read the reviews. A £40 air fryer that breaks after 3 months is more expensive than an £80 air fryer that lasts 5 years. Sort Amazon reviews by 'Most Recent' and read the 2-, 3-, and 4-star reviews — they're usually more informative than the 5-star raves and 1-star rants.
