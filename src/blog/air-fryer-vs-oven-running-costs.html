Air Fryer vs Oven: Which Is Cheaper to Run? (UK 2026)

We crunched the numbers using October 2026 energy price cap rates. Here's exactly how much each appliance costs per meal — and how much you could save over a year.

📅 13 May 2026 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ Home & Kitchen UK

💰 The Short Answer

An air fryer costs roughly 3× less to run than a conventional electric oven for most everyday meals. Cooking a portion of chips costs about 8p in an air fryer vs 24p in an oven. Over a year of daily use, switching from oven to air fryer saves the average UK household between £58 and £88 on electricity — enough to pay for the air fryer itself within 12–18 months.

UK Energy Prices in 2026: The Context

Before we dive into the numbers, let's establish what a unit of electricity actually costs. Under the Ofgem energy price cap for October–December 2026, the average unit rate for electricity (paid by direct debit) is 24.86p per kilowatt-hour (kWh). The standing charge is 60.99p per day, but we'll focus on the unit rate since that's what varies with usage.

⚡ Key Figure

Electricity unit rate: 24.86p per kWh (Ofgem cap, October 2026). Every 1,000 watts running for one hour costs 24.86p.

Gas is significantly cheaper at ~6.24p/kWh, so if you have a gas oven, the cost difference vs an air fryer narrows — but most UK ovens built in the last 20 years are electric.

An air fryer reaches temperature in 2–3 minutes and cooks faster because the hot air circulates more efficiently in a small space.

Why the Air Fryer Wins Every Time

1. No Preheat Penalty

A conventional oven burns through ~0.33 kWh just getting up to temperature — that's 8.3p before you've even put your food in. An air fryer's preheat phase is negligible: by the time you've opened the drawer, added your food, and closed it again, it's already at temperature.

2. Smaller Cavity = Faster Cooking

An air fryer basket has a volume of roughly 4–9 litres. An oven cavity is typically 60–80 litres. The air fryer's concentrated heat and powerful fan mean food cooks 20–30% faster than in an oven, even at the same temperature setting. Chips that take 25 minutes in an oven are done in 15–18 minutes in an air fryer.

3. No Wasted Heat

Every time you open an oven door, you lose about 20% of the heat — and the element kicks back in to compensate. An air fryer's drawer design means minimal heat loss when checking food. And when you're cooking for one or two people, you're not heating an entire oven cavity just to warm up a handful of chips.

🔍 Did You Know?

Research by Utilita Energy found that the average UK household uses their oven for just 37 minutes per day but spends around £57 per year on oven running costs alone. Switching even half of your oven usage to an air fryer, microwave, or slow cooker can cut that figure by 40–60%.

When an Oven Still Makes Sense

Air fryers aren't a replacement for every oven job. Here's when the oven wins:

Oven Advantages

  • Large roasts (turkey, leg of lamb) simply won't fit in most air fryers
  • Batch baking — 12 muffins at once vs 4 in an air fryer
  • Pizza — few air fryers fit a full 12-inch pizza
  • Slow, gentle cooking (casseroles, braises) over 2–3 hours

Air Fryer Limitations

  • Limited capacity: even large dual-basket models max out at ~11L
  • Not ideal for wet dishes (stews, soups)
  • Can dry out lean meats if not watched carefully
  • Single-layer cooking required for crispy results

The Annual Savings: Does an Air Fryer Pay for Itself?

Let's pull it all together. If you replace just one oven-cooked meal per day with air-fried equivalent:

  • Average saving per meal: ~16p (based on chips/fish fingers/chicken portions)
  • Daily saving: 16p
  • Annual saving: 365 × 16p = £58.40

If you're a family that uses the oven twice a day and replaces both with the air fryer, the saving doubles to around £116 per year.

Now consider the purchase price. A very good air fryer — the Tower T17088 Xpress Pro 11L — costs £89.99. At £58/year saving, it pays for itself in 18 months. A budget model like the Tefal Easy Fry 4.2L at £79.99 pays for itself even faster — about 16 months.

And that's before you factor in the oil savings (air fryers use little to no oil vs deep frying or even oven-roasting with oil), the time savings (faster cooking = less standing around), and the fact that an air fryer doesn't heat up your entire kitchen in summer.

Gas Ovens: A Different Calculation

If you have a gas oven, the maths changes. At ~6.24p/kWh for gas, a gas oven costs roughly 75% less to run than an electric oven for the same cooking time. Your 25p oven chips become ~6p on gas. However, gas ovens still need preheating, still take longer, and many UK homes (especially flats and new builds) have electric ovens. For those with gas ovens, the air fryer's advantages are more about speed and convenience than cost savings.

The Bottom Line

For the 8.4 million UK households with an electric oven (that's roughly 30% of homes according to the English Housing Survey), an air fryer is one of the quickest energy-saving investments you can make. It cooks faster, uses less electricity, produces better results for most everyday meals, and pays for itself within a year and a half.

If you're still on the fence, try this: use your air fryer exclusively for one week and check your smart meter. The difference will be visible.
