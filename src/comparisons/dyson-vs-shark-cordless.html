The Dyson V15 Detect is, objectively, the most advanced cordless vacuum you can buy in the UK in 2026. Its 240 AW suction is genuinely startling — it pulls fine dust out of carpets that look clean, and the piezo sensor counts particles as you clean, displaying them on the LCD screen in real time. Is that useful? It depends on your personality. If you find data satisfying, watching the particle count drop as you clean is weirdly addictive. If that sounds like nonsense to you, you'll ignore it after the first week.

The green laser on the fluffy head is the more practical feature. It projects a fan of green light at exactly the right angle to cast shadows from dust particles on hard floors, revealing debris you literally cannot see with the naked eye. On our test floor (a Victorian hallway that looked clean), the laser exposed a surprising amount of fine dust. It's not a gimmick — but it only works on hard floors, and after a few cleans, you'll know where the dusty spots are anyway.

The major ergonomic drawback: the trigger must be held continuously while vacuuming. For a quick 5-minute tidy-up, it's fine. For a full house clean (20+ minutes), your hand will ache. This is the single most common complaint in Dyson reviews, and Dyson has stubbornly refused to add an on/off switch.

  • Laser dust detection on hard floors is genuinely useful
  • Piezo particle counter with LCD display
  • Whole-machine HEPA filtration — excellent for allergy sufferers
  • Large 0.76L bin — fewer emptying stops

Cons

  • £549 — £200 more than the Shark Stratos for marginal extra performance

  • No anti hair-wrap — brush roll needs regular manual cleaning
  • No flexible wand — can't easily reach under low furniture

🔍 The 5 Differences That Actually Matter

1. The Trigger Problem (Dyson) vs On/Off Button (Shark)

This is the single most divisive design choice. Every Dyson cordless requires you to hold the trigger continuously while vacuuming. The logic: it saves battery by only running when you're actively cleaning. The reality: your hand and thumb ache after 15–20 minutes of continuous use. Shark uses a simple on/off button — press once to start, press again to stop. For anyone who cleans their whole house in one go, Shark's approach is noticeably more comfortable. For quick spot-cleans, Dyson's trigger is fine. If you have arthritis, carpal tunnel, or any hand/wrist issues, buy a Shark.

2. Anti Hair-Wrap: Shark's Genuine Advantage

If you have pets or long hair, this is the feature that may single-handedly decide your purchase. Shark's Anti Hair Wrap technology works. The brush roll has a flexible silicone fin and small comb that guides hair into the bin rather than letting it wind around the brush. In real-world testing with a Golden Retriever, the Shark brush roll remained clean; the Dyson needed manual hair removal after every full-house clean. Dyson's solution is to sell you a separate tangle-free turbine tool — which costs £60–£70 extra.

3. Flexible Wand (Shark) — The Under-Furniture Game-Changer

Shark's Flexology wand bends at the press of a button (or pedal, depending on model), letting you vacuum under sofas, beds, bookcases, and radiators without crouching, kneeling, or lying on the floor. Once you've used it, going back to a rigid Dyson wand feels primitive. Dyson offers no equivalent at any price.

4. Laser Dust Detection (Dyson) — Useful or Gimmick?

The Dyson V15's green laser (on the fluffy head) is genuinely useful on hard floors — it reveals dust you can't see with the naked eye, which is unsettling and satisfying in equal measure. The particle counter LCD is more gimmicky: fun for the first week, then ignored. Neither feature is available on the Dyson V8 or any Shark model. If you have predominantly hard floors and want the cleanest possible result, the laser is worth it. If you have mostly carpet, it's irrelevant.

5. Warranty: 5 Years (Shark) vs 2 Years (Dyson)

Shark's 5-year warranty (on both the Stratos and NZ801UKT) is more than double Dyson's 2-year warranty. For a product that gets daily use and has a battery that will degrade over time, this longer coverage provides genuine peace of mind. Dyson's customer service is generally good, but a longer warranty is objectively better.

🧭 Buying Guide: Dyson or Shark?

Choose Dyson if…

  • You want the absolute strongest suction available — the V15's 240 AW is unmatched
  • You have mostly hard floors and want the laser dust detection
  • You enjoy data/tech feedback (particle counter, LCD screen)
  • You're buying the V8 — at £249 it's the lightest, cheapest Dyson and still very good
  • You value clip-on swappable batteries (buy a spare for extended runtime)

Choose Shark if…

  • You have pets or long hair — anti hair-wrap is a genuine time-saver
  • You want the best value — Shark Stratos at £349 vs Dyson V15 at £549
  • You have lots of low furniture — the flexible wand is transformative
  • You find trigger fatigue annoying — Shark's on/off button is more comfortable
  • You want the longest warranty — 5 years provides real peace of mind
