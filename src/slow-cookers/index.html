How to Choose the Right Slow Cooker

Whether you're a slow-cooking novice or upgrading an old faithful, these are the key factors to weigh up before you buy.

Capacity: What Size Do You Need?

Size is the most important decision. Too small and you'll be cooking in batches; too large and you're wasting energy heating empty space.

  • 1-2 people: A 1.5-2.5 litre slow cooker is plenty. Look for compact models like the Crock-Pot 1.8L or Russell Hobbs 2L.
  • 3-4 people: 3.5 litres is the UK sweet spot. The Morphy Richards 460016, Crock-Pot CSC026, and Russell Hobbs 25630 all hit this mark perfectly.
  • 5+ people or batch cooking: Go for 5.5-6.5 litres. The Ninja Foodi PossibleCooker Pro at 4.7L straddles this category and can handle a 2kg joint with room to spare.

Ceramic vs Non-Stick Pot

Ceramic pots (found on the Morphy Richards, Crock-Pot, Russell Hobbs, and Lakeland models) are the traditional choice. They're heavy, retain heat beautifully, and can go straight to the table for serving. They're naturally non-stick when properly seasoned and won't scratch. Non-stick aluminium pots (like the Ninja PossibleCooker) are lighter, heat up faster, and are easier to handle, but the coating may eventually wear. Both are excellent — ceramic wins for longevity and heat distribution, non-stick for convenience and weight.

Energy Costs: The Slow Cooker Revival

There's a reason slow cookers are flying off UK shelves in 2026. With energy prices remaining high, slow cookers have emerged as the hero appliance of the cost-of-living era. Here's the maths:

  • Slow cooker (150W, 8 hours): ~1.2 kWh = approximately 30 pence for a full family meal
  • Electric oven (2500W, 2 hours): ~5 kWh = approximately £1.25 for the same meal
  • Annual saving: Switching from oven to slow cooker for just two meals per week saves roughly £100 per year on electricity.

Slow cookers also make cheaper cuts of meat tender and delicious — brisket, shin, and shoulder cuts that cost a fraction of prime cuts become restaurant-quality after eight hours on Low. This double saving (energy + ingredients) is driving the slow cooker renaissance.

Features: What's Actually Useful?

Manufacturers love to add features, but not all of them matter day to day. Here's what to look for:

  • Keep Warm: Essential. Automatically holds food at a safe serving temperature after cooking finishes. All five of our picks have this.
  • Auto Mode: Very useful. Starts on High to bring food to temperature, then drops to Low. Found on the Morphy Richards and Lakeland.
  • Timer: Nice to have. Lets you set an exact cooking duration, after which the cooker switches to Keep Warm. The Ninja PossibleCooker Pro has this; basic models don't.
  • Sear/Sauté: Game-changing if you want true one-pot cooking. The Ninja PossibleCooker Pro is the only model here with this capability.
  • Lockable Lid: Useful if you take your slow cooker to gatherings or potlucks. None of our picks include this, but it's worth considering if you transport food regularly.
