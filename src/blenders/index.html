How to Choose the Right Blender

From personal smoothie makers to commercial-grade powerhouses, here's what to consider before buying your next blender.

Jug Blender vs Personal Blender: Which Is Right For You?

The biggest decision in blender shopping is which format suits your lifestyle.

  • Jug blenders (Ninja Professional Plus, Vitamix, Sage Super Q) have a large pitcher — 1.4 to 2.1 litres. They're designed for family-sized batches of smoothies, soups, sauces, and frozen cocktails. They're more powerful, more versatile, and better at crushing ice and making hot soup. The tradeoff: they're bigger, louder, and more expensive.
  • Personal blenders (NutriBullet 600, Breville Blend Active) use smaller cups that double as drinking bottles. They're perfect for single-serve smoothies, compact enough for any kitchen, and typically cheaper. The tradeoff: they can't handle large batches, hot soup, or heavy-duty tasks like nut butter.

If you're a daily smoothie drinker cooking for one or two, a personal blender makes sense. If you cook for a family and want to make soups, sauces, and batch smoothies, a jug blender is the better investment.

Power: How Many Watts Do You Need?

Blender wattage translates directly to capability — but more isn't always better for everyone.

  • 300-600W: Ideal for soft fruits, yogurt, protein shakes, and light blending. The Breville Blend Active (300W) and NutriBullet 600 (600W) sit here.
  • 800-1200W: The sweet spot for most UK households. Handles frozen fruit, ice, fibrous greens, and nuts comfortably. The Ninja Professional Plus (1200W) is the standout here.
  • 1200W+: Required for crushing large quantities of ice, grinding nuts into butter, making hot soup from raw ingredients, and blending tough doughs. The Vitamix (1400W) and Sage Super Q (2400W) deliver professional-level results.

For reference, if you want to make a frozen margarita with properly crushed ice, you need at least 1000W. If you mostly make fresh fruit smoothies, 600W is plenty.

Glass vs Plastic Jugs

This is a genuinely important decision that affects durability, weight, and long-term appearance.

  • Glass jugs: Heavier and more stable on the base. Won't scratch, stain, or absorb odours. Look pristine for years. They can shatter if dropped on a hard floor. Found mostly on traditional blenders and some premium models like the KitchenAid Artisan K400.
  • Tritan plastic jugs: Lightweight, virtually unbreakable, and dishwasher-safe. Used by all five models in our roundup. Tritan is a high-grade BPA-free copolyester that resists clouding and staining far better than standard plastics. It may eventually develop micro-scratches after years of use, but for most UK kitchens, it's the practical choice.
