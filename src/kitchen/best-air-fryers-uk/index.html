How to Choose an Air Fryer

Capacity: How Many People Are You Cooking For?

1-2 people: A 4-5L basket is plenty. You can cook two portions of chips or two chicken breasts comfortably.

3-4 people: Aim for 5-6L. This fits a whole chicken or a family portion of roast potatoes.

5+ people: Either a dual-zone model (like the Ninja AF400UK) or cooking in batches.

Power: Faster Cooking = Higher Wattage

Most air fryers range from 1400W to 2500W. Higher wattage means faster preheating and crispier results — but also higher electricity draw. At the current UK energy price cap (24.5p/kWh), running a 1750W air fryer for 30 minutes costs about 21p — versus roughly 50p for a full oven.

Features That Actually Matter

  • Max temperature: 240°C models (like the Ninja) produce noticeably crispier results than 200°C models.
  • Dishwasher-safe parts: You'll clean it daily. This matters more than Wi-Fi.
  • Preset programs: Nice but not essential. Most people settle on 2-3 temperatures they use regularly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are air fryers cheaper to run than ovens?

Yes — significantly. An air fryer typically uses 1,500-1,800W and cooks food in roughly half the time of a conventional oven (2,000-3,000W). A typical air fryer meal costs 15-25p in electricity versus 40-60p in an oven.

Can you cook raw meat in an air fryer?

Absolutely. Chicken breasts, thighs, sausages, bacon, and even whole roast chickens cook beautifully in an air fryer. Use a meat thermometer to ensure safe internal temperatures.

Do you need to preheat an air fryer?

Most models recommend 2-3 minutes of preheating. In practice, we found preheating makes a noticeable difference for frozen foods and anything you want properly crispy. For reheating leftovers, it's not necessary.

Can air fryers replace a microwave?

Not completely. Air fryers are excellent for reheating foods that should be crispy (pizza, chips, pastry) but poor for foods that need moist reheating (soups, stews, rice). They complement a microwave rather than replace it.
