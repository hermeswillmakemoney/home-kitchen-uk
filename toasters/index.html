How to Choose the Right Toaster

Whether you're tired of unevenly browned toast or upgrading from a hand-me-down that's older than your children, understanding the key features will help you choose a toaster you'll love using every morning.

2-Slot vs 4-Slot: What's Right for Your Household?

This is the most important decision you'll make — and it comes down to how many people you regularly make toast for.

  • 1-2 people: A 2-slot toaster is perfectly adequate, takes up less worktop space, and costs less. The Smeg TSF01 (2 slots, £149.99) is the style choice, the Cuisinart SimpliTouch (2 slots, £59.99) brings modern tech, and the Russell Hobbs Groove (2 slots, £39.99) is the budget champion.
  • 3+ people or families: A 4-slot toaster saves real time in the morning rush. Instead of doing two batches back-to-back, you can toast for everyone in one go. Our 4-slot picks are the Dualit Newgen (£119.99) and Sage The Smart Toast (£129.99).

Also consider independent control: the Dualit Newgen lets you set different browning levels for each pair of slots, so one person's lightly toasted sourdough doesn't hold up someone else's well-done crumpets.

Slot Width: Think Beyond Sliced White

Standard toaster slots are around 28-30mm wide — fine for supermarket sliced bread, but a struggle for anything thicker. If you regularly buy artisan loaves, sourdough, or hand-cut bread, look for extra-wide slots (36mm+).

  • Sage The Smart Toast: 38mm slots — the widest in our roundup. Handles chunky sourdough with ease.
  • Smeg TSF01: 36mm self-centring racks hold thick slices evenly.
  • Cuisinart SimpliTouch: 36mm wide slots at a more accessible price point.
  • Dualit Newgen: Generous slots with a manual ejector for jammed bread.

The self-centring racks on the Smeg TSF01 and Sage The Smart Toast are particularly valuable for thick bread — they hold the slice upright and centred regardless of thickness, ensuring both sides toast evenly.

Browning Consistency: The Mark of a Great Toaster

The single biggest difference between a cheap toaster and a quality one is how evenly it browns bread. In our testing, we toasted six consecutive batches of identical white bread at the same setting and measured the colour across each slice.

The Sage The Smart Toast was the standout performer, delivering near-perfect consistency batch after batch thanks to its auto-adjusting heating elements. The Dualit Newgen followed closely, with its ProHeat elements producing reliably even results. The Russell Hobbs Groove punched well above its price point, with the grooved slots significantly reducing the pale-centre problem that plagues budget toasters.

If you're the sort of person who notices — and is annoyed by — toast with a pale centre and burnt edges, invest in a toaster that takes browning consistency seriously.

Essential Features: What Actually Matters?

Modern toasters come with an array of buttons and settings. Here's what's genuinely useful:

  • Defrost: A must-have if you keep bread in the freezer. It gently thaws the slice before toasting, preventing a soggy centre.
  • Reheat: A quick 30-second burst to warm up toast that's gone cold. More useful than you'd think.
  • Bagel: Toasts the cut side while gently warming the outside. Essential for bagel lovers.
  • Lift & Look: A feature on the Sage The Smart Toast that lets you raise the bread mid-cycle to check browning without cancelling. Brilliant for dialling in your perfect shade.
  • A Bit More: Another Sage exclusive — adds extra toasting time if your bread needs a little more colour. No need to start the cycle again.
  • High-Lift Lever: Found on the Russell Hobbs Groove and Dualit Newgen. Pushes smaller items higher so you can grab them without scorched fingertips.
  • Countdown Timer: The Cuisinart SimpliTouch displays remaining seconds. A small thing, but it eliminates the waiting-around-aimlessly factor.

Build Quality and Longevity

Toasters are among the most frequently replaced kitchen appliances. The average cheap toaster lasts 2-3 years before the heating elements fail or the mechanism jams. If you want a toaster that will still be performing a decade from now, look for:

  • Replaceable elements: The Dualit Newgen is the only toaster in our roundup with user-replaceable heating elements. When they eventually wear out, you can replace them rather than binning the whole appliance.
  • Metal construction: The Smeg TSF01 and Sage The Smart Toast use predominantly metal bodies that withstand daily use better than plastic.
  • Mechanical controls: The Dualit Newgen uses a mechanical timer rather than digital electronics. Fewer things to go wrong over the long term.
